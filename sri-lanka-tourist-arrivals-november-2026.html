Sri Lanka surpasses 1.9 million
tourist arrivals in 2026

The island has welcomed more than 1.9 million international visitors so far this year, with India leading the way. But the latest figures reveal a more complicated picture of the country’s
tourism recovery.

Sri Lanka has passed an important tourism milestone, recording more than 1.9 million international tourist arrivals between January 1 and November 8, 2026.

According to the Sri Lanka Tourism Development Authority (SLTDA), a total of 1,912,233 visitors arrived during this period. Nearly 50,000 tourists visited the island in the first eight days of November alone.

India remains the leading source market

India continues to be Sri Lanka’s largest source of international visitors, accounting for 512,026 arrivals so far this year.

The United Kingdom follows with 176,670 visitors, while China ranks third with 126,326 arrivals. Germany has also remained an important market, contributing 108,754 visitors.

These figures highlight the importance of both regional travellers and long-haul international markets to Sri Lanka’s tourism industry.

October brings
encouraging results

October was another positive month for the island’s tourism sector.

Sri Lanka welcomed 168,625 international visitors during the month, representing a 2.1% increase compared with October 2025.

The daily average reached approximately 5,440 arrivals, showing continued international interest in the destination.

A milestone, but not the whole story

Despite passing the 1.9 million mark, Sri Lanka’s cumulative tourist arrivals remain approximately 1.7% below the corresponding period in 2025.

This suggests that, although tourism continues to attract international visitors, the industry still has ground to make up.

The coming winter season will be particularly important, as Sri Lanka traditionally attracts visitors looking for warm weather, tropical beaches, wildlife experiences and cultural discoveries.

What does this mean
for travellers?

For visitors planning a trip, these figures offer an interesting glimpse into the changing popularity of Sri Lanka.

India remains the strongest source market, while travellers from Europe, China and other parts of Asia continue to contribute to the island’s international
tourism scene.

If you are planning to visit during the coming season, from December to March, it is worth booking accommodation, transport and popular experiences early, especially along the west and south coasts and in the hill country. Travelling outside the busiest weeks, such as the Christmas and New Year period, can also mean more choice and a calmer experience.

Beyond the numbers, the challenge for Sri Lanka will be to turn growing international interest into sustainable tourism that benefits local communities, small businesses and destinations across the island.
